Reading time and comprehension: a synthetic study
Collie Writer tutorial, 2026

This is invented teaching material for practicing nonfiction research and critique. No real participants were studied. The data, study and results must not be cited as actual findings.

Question: Does ten minutes of daily reading improve comprehension scores?

Illustrative method: Twelve volunteers completed the same ten-question comprehension exercise before and after two weeks of daily reading. There was no comparison group, random assignment or blinded scoring. Volunteers reported their own reading time.

Illustrative result: The mean score increased from 6 out of 10 to 8 out of 10. Individual scores, variation and missing-session records were not reported.

Claim to examine: The daily reading routine caused the improvement.

Limitations: Repeating the same exercise could produce a practice effect. Volunteers may differ from other readers. The small sample, absent comparison group and incomplete reporting prevent this example from isolating the effect of the reading routine.

Research task: Keep the observation separate from the causal claim. Select an exact excerpt, state what it supports or challenges, and identify independent evidence that would be needed. A critique should represent the claim fairly and record uncertainty.
